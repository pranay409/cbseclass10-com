Introduction to Trigonometry for CBSE Class 10: Ratios and Identities

Published on November 24, 2026

Trigonometry is one of the most important and scoring chapters in CBSE Class 10 Mathematics. It introduces the relationship between angles and sides of triangles, forming the foundation for advanced mathematics and physics. The NCERT Class 10 Maths Chapter 8 covers trigonometric ratios, their applications, and fundamental identities that frequently appear in board exams. Approximately 15-20% of the CBSE Class 10 Maths paper consists of trigonometry questions, making it crucial for students targeting high scores.

Understanding Trigonometric Ratios (NCERT Chapter 8.1-8.2)

Trigonometric ratios are mathematical functions that relate the angles of a right-angled triangle to the ratios of its sides. In a right-angled triangle, we define six basic trigonometric ratios for a given acute angle θ. These ratios form the backbone of trigonometry.

The six primary trigonometric ratios are:

sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent
cosec θ = Hypotenuse / Opposite
sec θ = Hypotenuse / Adjacent
cot θ = Adjacent / Opposite

A popular mnemonic to remember the first three ratios is SOH-CAH-TOA: Sin=Opposite/Hypotenuse, Cos=Adjacent/Hypotenuse, Tan=Opposite/Adjacent.

The reciprocal relationships are equally important:

  • cosec θ = 1/sin θ
  • sec θ = 1/cos θ
  • cot θ = 1/tan θ

Board exams frequently test these definitions through problems where students must identify and calculate ratios from given triangle diagrams. Understanding the positioning of opposite and adjacent sides relative to the given angle is crucial to avoid calculation errors.

Trigonometric Ratios of Standard Angles (NCERT Chapter 8.3)

CBSE Class 10 students must memorize the trigonometric values for standard angles: 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, and 90°. These values appear repeatedly in board exam questions and form the basis for solving complex trigonometry problems.

Angle: 0° → sin=0, cos=1, tan=0
Angle: 30° → sin=1/2, cos=√3/2, tan=1/√3
Angle: 45° → sin=1/√2, cos=1/√2, tan=1
Angle: 60° → sin=√3/2, cos=1/2, tan=√3
Angle: 90° → sin=1, cos=0, tan=undefined
⚠️ Common Mistake: Students often confuse the values of sin 30° and sin 60°. Remember: sin 30° = 1/2 (smaller angle, smaller value) and sin 60° = √3/2 (larger angle, larger value). Similarly, cos follows the reverse pattern.

These standard values are tested through numerical problems where substitution is required. Many multi-step problems in the board exam rely on students' ability to quickly and accurately recall these values without calculator assistance.

Trigonometric Identities (NCERT Chapter 8.4)

Trigonometric identities are equations involving trigonometric functions that hold true for all values of the variables involved. The NCERT curriculum focuses on three fundamental identities that students must prove and apply.

Identity 1: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
Identity 2: 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
Identity 3: 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

The first identity, sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, is derived from the Pythagorean theorem and is the most frequently used in both CBSE Class 10 and competitive exams. The other two identities can be derived from the first by dividing both sides by cos²θ and sin²θ respectively.

Board exams test these identities through proof-based questions and application problems. Students are often asked to:

  • Prove given trigonometric expressions using identities
  • Simplify complex trigonometric expressions
  • Find unknown trigonometric values given one ratio and an angle range
  • Verify identity equations for specific angle values

Mastery of these three identities is essential as they cannot be solved by memorization alone—students must understand their algebraic manipulations and logical derivations to apply them correctly in unfamiliar problem contexts.

Exam Pattern and Problem-Solving Strategy (NCERT Application)

In the CBSE Class 10 Maths board exam, trigonometry typically accounts for 1-2 questions of 2 marks each and 1 question of 4 marks. The question distribution follows these patterns:

  • 2-Mark Questions: Finding trigonometric ratios from diagrams, calculating values using standard angles, simplifying expressions using identities
  • 4-Mark Questions: Proving complex identities, multi-step trigonometric problems, applications involving heights and distances (covered in Chapter 9)

To score maximum marks, students should follow this strategy: First, clearly identify the given angle and triangle sides. Second, choose the appropriate trigonometric ratio based on the given and required sides. Third, apply standard angle values or identities systematically. Finally, verify the answer by substituting back or checking dimensional consistency.
