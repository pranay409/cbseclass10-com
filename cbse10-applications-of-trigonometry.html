Some Applications of Trigonometry for CBSE Class 10: Heights and Distances

Published on November 25, 2026

Trigonometry is not just an abstract mathematical concept confined to textbooks. It has real-world applications that solve practical problems we encounter daily. For CBSE Class 10 students, Chapter 9 (Some Applications of Trigonometry) is crucial as it bridges the gap between theoretical trigonometric ratios and their practical use in determining heights and distances. This chapter typically carries 4-6 marks in the board exam and focuses on practical word problems involving angles of elevation and depression.

Understanding Angles of Elevation and Depression

The foundation of applications of trigonometry lies in understanding two key concepts: angle of elevation and angle of depression.

Angle of Elevation

When an observer looks up at an object above the horizontal level of their eye, the angle formed between the horizontal line and the line of sight is called the angle of elevation. For example, when you stand on the ground and look up at the top of a building, the angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal is the angle of elevation.

Angle of Depression

Conversely, when an observer looks down at an object below the horizontal level of their eye, the angle formed between the horizontal line and the line of sight is called the angle of depression. For instance, if you stand on a cliff and look down at a boat in the sea, the angle is an angle of depression.

Important Note: Due to alternate angles, the angle of elevation from point A to point B equals the angle of depression from point B to point A.

Key Concepts and Problem-Solving Approach (NCERT Chapter 9)

To solve heights and distances problems effectively, follow this systematic approach:

Step 1: Draw a Diagram

Always sketch a clear diagram representing the problem. Mark all given information including angles, known distances, and the quantity to find. This visual representation is essential and often the difference between solving and failing to solve a problem.

Step 2: Identify the Right Triangle

Heights and distances problems invariably form right triangles. Identify which angle is 90°, which angle is given (angle of elevation or depression), and which side is known or unknown.

Step 3: Apply Appropriate Trigonometric Ratios

Use the ratios sine, cosine, and tangent depending on which sides of the triangle are involved:

  • sin θ = Opposite/Hypotenuse - Use when you know/need opposite side and hypotenuse
  • cos θ = Adjacent/Hypotenuse - Use when you know/need adjacent side and hypotenuse
  • tan θ = Opposite/Adjacent - Use when only opposite and adjacent sides are involved (most common in heights and distances)

Step 4: Solve and Verify

Substitute values and solve the equation. Always verify your answer makes practical sense (heights cannot be negative, distances should be reasonable).

⚠️ Common Mistake Alert

Many students confuse angle of elevation with angle of depression. Remember: Elevation = Looking UP, Depression = Looking DOWN. Also, don't forget to include the observer's height when calculating total height of an object!

Real-World Applications and Board Exam Patterns

CBSE board exams typically feature 2-3 questions from this chapter in the question paper. These questions usually involve scenarios like:

  • Tower and Building Heights: A person at a certain distance from a building observes the top at an angle of elevation. Find the height.
  • Observer on Heights: A person on a building/tower/cliff looks down at an object. Find horizontal distance using angle of depression.
  • Multiple Objects: Problems involving two buildings or objects at different heights.
  • Moving Observer: As an observer moves, angles change. These require solving two equations simultaneously.
  • Practical Measurements: Finding heights of mountains, trees, or poles using clinometers or similar instruments.

Exam Pattern Analysis

Based on recent CBSE papers (2022-2024), applications of trigonometry questions follow this pattern:

  • Short Answer Questions (2 marks): Simple single-step problems with one angle and one distance given
  • Long Answer Questions (4 marks): Complex problems with multiple angles or objects, requiring two or more trigonometric equations

Important Formulas and Standard Results

While trigonometric ratios remain your primary tool, remembering these helps:

  • For angle of 30°: tan 30° = 1/√3, sin 30° = 1/2, cos 30° = √3/2
  • For angle of 45°: tan 45° = 1, sin 45° = 1/√2, cos 45° = 1/√2
  • For angle of 60°: tan 60° = √3, sin 60° = √3/2, cos 60° = 1/2
  • Pythagoras Theorem: h² = a² + b² (useful for verification)

Master these special angles as 90% of board exam questions use them, making calculations manageable without calculators.
