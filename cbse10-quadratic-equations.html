Quadratic Equations for CBSE Class 10: Factorisation and Formula Method

Published on November 20, 2026 | Updated for 2024-2025 Exam Pattern

Quadratic Equations are one of the most important topics in CBSE Class 10 Mathematics, appearing consistently in board exams with 4-6 marks allocation. Chapter 4 of the NCERT Class 10 Maths textbook is dedicated entirely to this fundamental concept that forms the foundation for higher mathematics. This comprehensive guide will help you master both the factorisation method and the quadratic formula—the two primary solution methods you'll encounter in your board examination.

Understanding Quadratic Equations (NCERT Chapter 4)

A quadratic equation is a polynomial equation of the second degree, written in the standard form:

ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0

Here, a, b, and c are real numbers, and a must be non-zero (otherwise, it wouldn't be quadratic). The CBSE board exam typically tests your understanding through three main solution methods: factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula.

According to the NCERT curriculum, students must understand:

  • The nature of roots (real and distinct, real and equal, or imaginary) using the discriminant
  • How to convert word problems into quadratic equations
  • Application of quadratic equations in real-world scenarios
  • Relationship between roots and coefficients (Vieta's formulas)
⚠️ Common Mistake Students Make: Many students forget to check that a ≠ 0 before claiming an equation is quadratic. Also, always rearrange the equation to standard form (ax² + bx + c = 0) before applying any solution method. This simple step prevents 90% of calculation errors in board exams!

Method 1: Solving by Factorisation

Factorisation is the quickest method when the quadratic equation can be easily split into two linear factors. This method is particularly favored in board exams because it requires minimal calculation and is less prone to errors.

Step-by-Step Process:

  1. Write the equation in standard form: ax² + bx + c = 0
  2. Find two numbers that multiply to give (a × c) and add to give b
  3. Split the middle term using these two numbers
  4. Factor by grouping
  5. Set each factor equal to zero and solve for x

Example: Solve x² + 7x + 12 = 0

Here, a = 1, b = 7, c = 12. We need two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7. These numbers are 3 and 4.

Therefore: x² + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4) = 0

This gives us x = -3 or x = -4

The factorisation method is highly recommended for board exams when the discriminant is a perfect square. According to the NCERT textbook analysis, approximately 40% of board exam questions can be solved using this method.

Method 2: The Quadratic Formula (Sridharacharya Formula)

When factorisation isn't possible or is too complex, the quadratic formula—also known as the Sridharacharya formula in India—is your reliable solution. This formula works for every quadratic equation and is essential for board exam preparation.

x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / (2a)

Using the Discriminant (Δ):

The discriminant Δ = b² - 4ac determines the nature of roots:

  • If Δ > 0: Two distinct real roots
  • If Δ = 0: One real root (or two equal real roots)
  • If Δ < 0: No real roots (complex roots)

Example: Solve 2x² - 8x + 6 = 0

Here, a = 2, b = -8, c = 6

Δ = (-8)² - 4(2)(6) = 64 - 48 = 16

Since Δ > 0, we have two distinct real roots:

x = [8 ± √16] / 4 = [8 ± 4] / 4

Therefore: x = 3 or x = 1

NCERT emphasizes that students must practice recognizing when to use which method. In board exams, questions testing the quadratic formula often appear with irrational coefficients, making factorisation impossible.

Board Exam Patterns & Important Concepts

Based on previous CBSE board exam analysis (2020-2025), here's what you need to know:

Exam Weightage:

  • Short Answer Questions (2 marks): Simple equations solvable by factorisation
  • Long Answer Questions (4 marks): Word problems requiring equation formation and solution
  • Application-based Questions: Real-world scenarios like area problems, speed-distance problems, or age-related problems

Relationship Between Roots and Coefficients:

If α and β are roots of ax² + bx + c = 0, then:

  • Sum of roots: α + β = -b/a
  • Product of roots: αβ = c/a

This concept frequently appears in higher-order questions and is crucial for solving advanced problems in one step.

✓ Board Exam Tip: Always verify your solutions by substituting them back into the original equation. This verification step takes only 30 seconds but can save you marks if you've made a calculation error. Additionally, many board exam questions ask you to verify that your solutions satisfy a given condition—so this habit is essential!
