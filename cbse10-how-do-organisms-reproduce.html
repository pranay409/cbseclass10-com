How Do Organisms Reproduce for CBSE Class 10

Published: November 09, 2026

Chapter 8 of the CBSE Class 10 Science curriculum, "How Do Organisms Reproduce?", is a fundamental topic that explores the biological processes through which living organisms create offspring and ensure the continuation of their species. This chapter is essential for board exams and typically carries 5-8 marks in the final examination. Understanding reproduction mechanisms is crucial not only for clearing board exams but also for building strong foundational knowledge in biology.

1. Introduction to Reproduction (NCERT Section 8.1)

Reproduction is one of the key characteristics of living organisms. It is the biological process by which new individual organisms are produced from one or more parent organisms. The continuity of species depends entirely on reproduction. In the CBSE Class 10 curriculum, reproduction is classified into two main types: asexual reproduction and sexual reproduction.

For board examination purposes, students must understand that reproduction is not essential for the survival of an individual organism, but it is essential for the survival of species. This distinction is often tested in short-answer questions (2-3 marks) in the CBSE Class 10 board exams.

Why is Reproduction Important?

  • Ensures survival and continuation of species
  • Increases population of organisms
  • Helps in genetic variation (in sexual reproduction)
  • Allows organisms to adapt to changing environments

2. Asexual Reproduction (NCERT Section 8.2)

Asexual reproduction is the process of reproduction involving only one parent organism. No gamete fusion occurs, and the offspring are genetically identical to the parent (clones). This type of reproduction is common in plants, bacteria, and simple animals. The CBSE board exams frequently ask students to differentiate between asexual and sexual reproduction with specific examples.

Types of Asexual Reproduction:

1. Binary Fission: This is seen in bacteria and some protozoa where a single organism divides into two identical organisms. The parent cell's DNA replicates and the cell divides, producing two daughter cells. This is a crucial topic for 3-5 mark questions in board exams.

2. Budding: In this process, a small bud develops on the parent organism, eventually detaches, and becomes a new individual. Hydra reproduces through budding, and this example is frequently tested in CBSE Class 10 examinations.

3. Fragmentation: The parent organism breaks into small fragments, each capable of growing into a new individual. Planaria (flatworm) reproduces through fragmentation and is a standard example in the NCERT textbook.

4. Regeneration: The ability to regrow lost body parts. In some cases, this process can lead to the formation of new organisms. Starfish can regenerate lost arms and grow new bodies from these fragments.

5. Spore Formation: Fungi and algae produce spores that develop into new organisms without fertilization. Bread mold (Rhizopus) is a classic example discussed in NCERT.

6. Vegetative Propagation in Plants: Plants reproduce asexually through runners, tubers, bulbs, and corms. Potatoes (tubers), onions (bulbs), and strawberries (runners) are important examples that appear in board exams.

3. Sexual Reproduction (NCERT Section 8.3-8.9)

Sexual reproduction involves two parent organisms and the fusion of male and female gametes (sex cells). This process results in genetic variation among offspring, making them unique from each other and from their parents. Sexual reproduction is more complex than asexual reproduction but offers significant evolutionary advantages.

Key Concepts in Sexual Reproduction:

Gamete Formation: Sexual reproduction begins with the formation of gametes through meiosis. In animals, this occurs in specialized reproductive organs: testes in males and ovaries in females. Each gamete carries half the genetic information (haploid), and when two gametes fuse, the resulting zygote has the full complement of chromosomes (diploid).

Fertilization: This is the fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote. In animals, fertilization can be external (in water) or internal (within the body). The CBSE board exams test the understanding of these different fertilization methods with examples such as frogs (external) and humans (internal).

Embryonic Development: After fertilization, the zygote undergoes repeated cell divisions (mitosis) to form an embryo. In mammals, the embryo develops inside the uterus, protected and nourished by the placenta and amnion. This intrauterine development ensures higher survival rates of offspring.

⚠️ Common Exam Mistake: Students often confuse mitosis (which produces identical cells for growth and repair) with meiosis (which produces gametes with half the chromosomes). Remember: Meiosis occurs during gamete formation, while mitosis occurs during growth and development of the embryo.

Human Reproductive System:

The CBSE Class 10 Science curriculum includes a detailed study of the human reproductive system. The male reproductive system produces sperm in the testes, while the female reproductive system produces eggs (ova) in the ovaries. The menstrual cycle in females, typically lasting 28 days, prepares the uterus for pregnancy. Board exams often include 5-mark questions on the menstrual cycle, fertilization, and embryonic development.

Key hormones involved include testosterone (in males), and estrogen and progesterone (in females). These hormones regulate the reproductive processes and are crucial for board exam preparation.

4. Reproduction in Plants (NCERT Section 8.8-8.9)

Plants exhibit both asexual and sexual reproduction. Sexual reproduction in plants involves the fusion of male and female gametes that occur in the flower. The flower is the reproductive organ of flowering plants, containing both male parts (stamens) and female parts (pistils). This section is crucial as it typically appears as a 3-5 mark question in board exams.

Pollination and Fertilization:

  • Pollination: Transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma, either by wind, water, insects, or birds.
  • Fertilization: Fusion of the male pollen nucleus with the female ovule nucleus, resulting in the formation of seeds.
  • Seed Dispersal: Seeds are dispersed by wind, water, or animals, ensuring the spread of species to new locations.

Students must understand the complete reproductive cycle of flowering plants, from flower structure to seed formation, as this is a common source of long-answer questions (5-8 marks) in the CBSE Class 10 board exams.

5. Exam Patterns and Important Topics for CBSE Class 10

Based on previous years' CBSE Class 10 board exams, this chapter typically contributes 5-8 marks to the question paper. Questions are generally framed as:

  • 1-2 Mark Questions: Define key terms like reproduction, gamete, zygote, or differentiate between asexual and sexual reproduction.
  • 3 Mark Questions: Describe the process of binary fission, budding, or explain the menstrual cycle.
  • 5 Mark Questions: Explain human reproductive systems in detail, describe flowering plant reproduction, or compare asexual and sexual reproduction with examples.

Diagram-based questions are also common. Students should be able to label diagrams of flowers, human reproductive systems, and different types of reproduction. Practice drawing and labeling these diagrams multiple times to ensure clarity during board exams.
