Acids, Bases and Salts for CBSE Class 10: Complete Chapter Guide

Published on November 03, 2026

Acids, Bases and Salts is one of the most fundamental chapters in CBSE Class 10 Chemistry (NCERT Chapter 2). This chapter builds the foundation for understanding chemical reactions, pH scale, and salt preparation, which are recurring themes in board exams. Whether you're appearing for your Class 10 Board Exams or building strong chemistry fundamentals, mastering this chapter is essential. Let's dive deep into the core concepts and exam-pattern questions.

Understanding Acids, Bases, and Salts: Definitions and Properties [NCERT 2.1-2.3]

What are Acids?

According to Arrhenius and Brønsted-Lowry theories, acids are substances that donate hydrogen ions (H⁺) in aqueous solutions. Common acids include hydrochloric acid (HCl), sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄), and acetic acid (CH₃COOH). Acids have characteristic properties such as sour taste, ability to turn blue litmus paper red, and corrosive nature.

What are Bases?

Bases are substances that accept hydrogen ions or donate hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in solutions. Common bases include sodium hydroxide (NaOH), potassium hydroxide (KOH), and ammonia (NH₃). Bases are bitter in taste, turn red litmus paper blue, and feel slippery to touch.

Difference Between Acids and Bases

Property Acids Bases
Taste Sour Bitter
Effect on Litmus Paper Blue → Red Red → Blue
pH Range Below 7 Above 7
Conductivity Good Good
Reaction with Bases Neutralization Neutralization

The pH Scale and Indicators [NCERT 2.4]

The pH scale measures the concentration of hydrogen ions in a solution, ranging from 0 to 14. A pH of 7 is neutral (pure water), values below 7 indicate acidity, and values above 7 indicate basicity.

pH Scale Breakdown

  • pH 0-6: Acidic solutions (0 being most acidic)
  • pH 7: Neutral solutions
  • pH 8-14: Basic/Alkaline solutions (14 being most basic)

Understanding the pH scale is crucial for board exams. Common questions ask students to identify whether a substance is acidic, basic, or neutral based on its pH value or observable properties.

Natural and Synthetic Indicators

Indicators are substances that change color in the presence of acids or bases. Natural indicators include turmeric (yellow in base, colorless in acid), phenolphthalein (colorless in acid, pink in base), and methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in base). These indicators are frequently used in practical exams and multiple-choice questions on the board examination.

⚠️ Common Exam Mistake

Many students confuse the terms "alkaline" and "basic." Remember: all bases are alkaline, but not all alkaline solutions are strong bases. Weak bases can also be alkaline. This distinction often appears in CBSE board exams as a 1-mark question.

Neutralization Reactions and Salt Formation [NCERT 2.5-2.6]

When an acid and a base react, they undergo a neutralization reaction to form salt and water. This is one of the most tested concepts in CBSE Class 10 Chemistry.

General Neutralization Equation

Acid + Base → Salt + Water

For example:

  • HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
  • H₂SO₄ + 2KOH → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O
  • HNO₃ + NH₃·H₂O → NH₄NO₃ + H₂O

Types of Salts

Salts can be classified based on the nature of the acid and base used to form them:

  • Normal Salts: Formed from complete neutralization (e.g., NaCl, K₂SO₄)
  • Acidic Salts: Formed when weak base is neutralized by excess strong acid (e.g., NaHSO₄)
  • Basic Salts: Formed when weak acid is neutralized by excess strong base (e.g., NaOCl)

Preparation of Salts

Board exams frequently ask about salt preparation methods. Common methods include:

  • By Direct Combination: 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl
  • By Neutralization: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
  • By Displacement: Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
  • By Oxidation-Reduction: Cu + 2H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + SO₂ + 2H₂O

💡 Key Exam Tip

In CBSE board exams, you may be asked to write balanced chemical equations for neutralization reactions. Always ensure the number of atoms on both sides of the equation is equal. Practice with strong acid-strong base, strong acid-weak base, and weak acid-strong base combinations to cover all exam patterns.

Salts in Everyday Life and Industrial Applications [NCERT 2.7]

Salts play a crucial role in our daily lives and have numerous industrial applications. CBSE exams often include questions about the uses and importance of common salts.

Common Salts and Their Applications

  • Sodium Chloride (NaCl): Used in food preservation, de-icing roads, and production of caustic soda and chlorine
  • Calcium Carbonate (CaCO₃): Used in antacids, cement production, and as a building material
  • Potassium Nitrate (KNO₃): Used in fertilizers and food preservation
