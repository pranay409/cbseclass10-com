Control and Coordination for CBSE Class 10: Nervous and Hormonal System

Published on November 08, 2026

Control and Coordination is a fundamental chapter in CBSE Class 10 Science (Biology) that explains how living organisms maintain homeostasis and respond to their environment. This chapter covers two major regulatory systems: the Nervous System and the Endocrine (Hormonal) System. Understanding these systems is crucial for board exam success, as they account for 3-5 marks in the CBSE Class 10 Science paper.

The Nervous System: Structure and Function (NCERT Chapter 7)

The nervous system is a complex network of specialized cells that transmit electrical and chemical signals throughout the body. It consists of the Central Nervous System (CNS) and the Peripheral Nervous System (PNS).

Central Nervous System: The CNS comprises the brain and spinal cord. The brain processes information and sends commands, while the spinal cord acts as a pathway between the brain and the rest of the body. For CBSE exams, students must understand that the brain controls all voluntary actions, and the spinal cord mediates reflexes without brain involvement.

Peripheral Nervous System: The PNS includes all nerves and ganglia outside the CNS. It has two divisions:

  • Somatic Nervous System: Controls voluntary actions like walking, writing, and speaking.
  • Autonomic Nervous System: Controls involuntary actions like heart rate, digestion, and breathing. It further divides into Sympathetic and Parasympathetic systems.

The Neuron: The basic functional unit of the nervous system is the neuron. Each neuron has a cell body, dendrites (receive signals), and an axon (transmits signals). Neurons communicate through synapses using neurotransmitters. Boards often ask students to label a neuron diagram or explain synaptic transmission—practice these thoroughly.

Reflex Arc: Rapid Response Mechanism (NCERT Chapter 7)

A reflex arc is an involuntary, automatic response to a stimulus that does not require conscious thought. When you touch a hot surface and immediately pull your hand away, that's a reflex action. The pathway is: Receptor → Sensory Neuron → Spinal Cord → Motor Neuron → Effector (muscle).

This is a board exam favorite. Students must understand:

  • Reflex arcs bypass the brain and involve only the spinal cord.
  • They are faster than conscious responses because the brain isn't involved in decision-making.
  • Common examples: knee-jerk reflex, touching a flame and withdrawing, pupil dilation in darkness.

Diagram-based questions on reflex arcs frequently appear in CBSE papers. Make sure you can draw and label the complete reflex arc pathway.

Common Exam Mistake: Many students confuse reflex actions with voluntary actions. Remember: reflex actions are automatic and don't involve conscious thought, while voluntary actions require brain processing. When asked "Is a reflex action voluntary?"—the answer is NO. This distinction is crucial for scoring full marks.

The Hormonal System: Chemical Coordination (NCERT Chapter 8)

While the nervous system provides rapid electrical communication, the endocrine system uses hormones (chemical messengers) for slower but longer-lasting regulation. Hormones are secreted by endocrine glands and travel through the bloodstream to target cells.

Key Endocrine Glands and Hormones:

  • Pituitary Gland: The "master gland" controls growth and reproduction. Anterior lobe secretes growth hormone (GH); posterior lobe secretes antidiuretic hormone (ADH).
  • Thyroid Gland: Produces thyroxine, regulating metabolism and growth.
  • Adrenal Glands: Secrete adrenaline (epinephrine), the "fight-or-flight" hormone.
  • Pancreas: Produces insulin and glucagon to regulate blood glucose levels.
  • Ovaries and Testes: Produce sex hormones (estrogen, testosterone) for reproduction.

Board exams frequently test knowledge of these glands. Expected questions include: "Which hormone regulates blood sugar?" (Answer: Insulin from pancreas), "What is the effect of adrenaline during an emergency?" (Answer: Increases heart rate, blood pressure, and glucose supply). Practice matching glands with their hormones—this is a common objective-type question.

Nervous System vs. Hormonal System: Key Differences (NCERT Chapters 7-8)

CBSE Class 10 exams often include comparison questions. Here are the critical differences:

  • Speed: Nervous system is faster (milliseconds); hormonal system is slower (minutes to hours).
  • Medium: Nervous system uses electrical impulses and neurotransmitters; hormonal system uses chemical hormones.
  • Duration: Nervous response is brief; hormonal effects are longer-lasting.
  • Range: Nervous system is localized; hormonal system affects the entire body through the bloodstream.
  • Example: Touching a hot stove (nervous—immediate reflex) vs. growth and development (hormonal—long-term process).

When answering comparison questions, structure your answer with clear headings or bullet points to show understanding of both systems. This approach often earns bonus marks for presentation in board exams.

Exam Tip: Questions on "Nervous and Hormonal Coordination" often ask how these systems work together. For example: "Explain the role of adrenaline during an emergency using both nervous and hormonal systems." Your answer should mention: sympathetic nervous system activation → adrenaline release from adrenal medulla → increased heart rate and blood pressure. This integration shows comprehensive understanding and secures maximum marks.

Practice Patterns for CBSE Exams

Based on recent CBSE papers (2022-2025), expect the following question formats:

  • 1-2 Mark Questions: Define neurons, name endocrine glands, distinguish between voluntary and involuntary actions.
  • 3 Mark Questions: Explain reflex arc with diagram, compare nervous and hormonal systems, describe the role of a specific hormone.
  • 5 Mark Questions: Detailed explanation of how CNS controls body functions, integrated response to stimuli involving both systems, case studies on hormone deficiency diseases.
  • Diagram Questions: Label neuron, synapse, brain regions, reflex arc pathways.

Revise the chapter systematically. First, understand concepts; second, memorize key glands and hormones; third, practice previous year board papers focusing on this chapter. Allocate 7-10 days for thorough preparation.
