Minerals and Energy Resources for CBSE Class 10: Complete Chapter Guide

Published on October 20, 2026

The chapter on Minerals and Energy Resources is a crucial part of the CBSE Class 10 Geography curriculum (NCERT Social Studies - Geography Chapter 5). This chapter focuses on understanding how minerals are formed, their types, distribution in India, and the various energy resources that power our nation. Board exams frequently test this topic through MCQs, short-answer questions, and location-based map work. Let's dive into a comprehensive study guide that will help you ace this chapter.

Understanding Minerals: Types and Formation (NCERT Foundation)

According to the NCERT textbook, a mineral is a naturally occurring inorganic solid with a definite chemical composition and an ordered atomic arrangement. Minerals are the building blocks of rocks and are essential for human survival and industrial development.

Classification of Minerals:

  • Metallic Minerals: Contain metals and are further divided into ferrous (containing iron like iron ore, manganese) and non-ferrous (copper, bauxite, tin, zinc).
  • Non-metallic Minerals: Do not contain metals (limestone, mica, salt, potash, phosphate).
  • Gemstones: Precious and semi-precious minerals (diamonds, rubies, emeralds).

Minerals form through various geological processes over millions of years. The NCERT emphasizes that understanding mineral distribution is crucial for identifying mineral-rich regions in India. Major mineral-producing regions include Chhattisgarh (coal and iron ore), Karnataka (gold and manganese), Odisha (iron ore and bauxite), and Jharkhand (coal and copper).

Distribution of Minerals in India: Key Exam Focus

The CBSE Class 10 board exam expects students to know the major mineral reserves and their locations across India. This section is perfect for map-based questions and location identification in the geography board exam.

Important Mineral Distributions:

  • Iron Ore: Primarily found in Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, and Karnataka. India is the world's largest exporter of iron ore.
  • Bauxite (Aluminium Ore): Concentrated in Odisha, Gujarat, Jharkhand, and Maharashtra.
  • Copper: Mainly extracted in Jharkhand, Rajasthan, and Karnataka.
  • Manganese: Found in Odisha, Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Madhya Pradesh.
  • Mica: India is the world's largest producer; found in Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh, and Rajasthan.
  • Salt: Produced in Gujarat, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, and Andhra Pradesh.

💡 Common Board Exam Mistake:

Students often confuse mineral-rich states. Remember: Odisha is rich in iron ore AND bauxite; Jharkhand is known for coal AND copper. Practice map-marking exercises to solidify your geographic knowledge. This accounts for 3-5 marks in map-based questions.

Energy Resources: Renewable vs. Non-Renewable (Critical for Scoring)

The NCERT Chapter 5 extensively covers energy resources, which is vital for sustainability topics in board exams. Energy resources are broadly classified into two categories:

Non-Renewable Energy Resources (Conventional):

  • Coal: India's primary energy source, with major reserves in Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Jharkhand, and West Bengal. Coal contributes over 50% of India's electricity generation.
  • Petroleum: Reserves found in the Arabian Sea, Assam, and Gujarat. India is a net importer of petroleum.
  • Natural Gas: Extracted from offshore fields in the Arabian Sea and Assam.

Renewable Energy Resources (Non-Conventional):

  • Solar Energy: India has vast potential; initiatives like the National Solar Mission promote solar power.
  • Wind Energy: Concentrated in Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, and Maharashtra.
  • Hydroelectric Power: Generated from dams on major rivers like the Brahmaputra, Godavari, and Krishna.
  • Geothermal and Biomass Energy: Emerging sources with growing potential.

Board exams frequently ask about sustainable development and why India is shifting towards renewable energy. Understanding the advantages (eco-friendly, sustainable) and disadvantages (high initial cost, land requirement) of each energy type is essential for scoring full marks in 5-mark questions.

Mining and Its Environmental Impact: A Holistic Approach

The NCERT textbook dedicates significant focus to the environmental consequences of mining. This reflects the modern geography board exam's emphasis on sustainable development and environmental geography.

Environmental Challenges from Mining:

  • Habitat destruction and loss of biodiversity
  • Soil degradation and pollution
  • Water contamination and depletion
  • Air pollution from dust and emissions
  • Social displacement of indigenous communities

The CBSE expects students to discuss sustainable mining practices such as reclamation of mined areas, controlled mining operations, and rehabilitation programs. These topics often appear as 3-mark or 5-mark descriptive questions in the board exam.
