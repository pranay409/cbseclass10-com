Coordinate Geometry for CBSE Class 10: Distance and Section Formula

Published: November 23, 2026

Coordinate Geometry is one of the most important chapters in CBSE Class 10 Mathematics, frequently appearing in board exams with 4-6 marks allocated to this topic. Understanding the Distance Formula and Section Formula is fundamental to solving real-world problems involving points, lines, and their relationships on a coordinate plane.

Understanding Coordinate Geometry: NCERT Chapter 7 Overview

(NCERT Class 10 Maths, Chapter 7: Coordinate Geometry)

Coordinate Geometry is the branch of mathematics that uses algebra to describe geometric shapes and their properties. It bridges the gap between pure geometry and algebra by representing points, lines, and other geometric figures using ordered pairs of numbers.

In CBSE Class 10, this chapter focuses on two core concepts:

  • Distance Formula: Calculating the distance between two points on a coordinate plane
  • Section Formula: Finding coordinates of a point that divides a line segment in a given ratio

These formulas are not just theoretical—they're practical tools used in engineering, physics, navigation, and computer graphics. Board exams typically test these formulas through direct application and reasoning questions.

Distance Formula: Finding Distance Between Two Points

(NCERT Class 10 Maths, Chapter 7, Section 7.2)

The Distance Formula is derived from the Pythagorean theorem and allows us to find the distance between any two points on a coordinate plane.

Distance Formula:
If P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂) are two points, then:
PQ = √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²]

Key Points to Remember

  • Always square both differences before adding them
  • Distance is always positive; never negative
  • Distance from origin (0, 0) to point P(x, y) is √(x² + y²)
  • The formula works for all quadrants

Common Board Exam Questions

Example 1: Find the distance between points A(3, 4) and B(6, 8).

Solution: Using the Distance Formula:
AB = √[(6-3)² + (8-4)²] = √[9 + 16] = √25 = 5 units

Example 2: Prove that the triangle with vertices A(0, 0), B(4, 0), and C(0, 3) is a right-angled triangle.

Approach: Calculate all three sides using the Distance Formula, then verify if Pythagoras theorem holds (sum of squares of two smaller sides = square of longest side).

⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid

Many students forget to square the differences before adding. The Distance Formula is NOT √(x₂ - x₁) + √(y₂ - y₁). Always calculate [(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²] first, then take the square root of the entire sum.

Section Formula: Dividing a Line Segment

(NCERT Class 10 Maths, Chapter 7, Section 7.3)

The Section Formula helps us find the coordinates of a point that divides a line segment joining two given points in a specified ratio.

Section Formula (Internal Division):
If point P divides the line segment joining A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) in the ratio m:n internally, then:
P = ( (m·x₂ + n·x₁)/(m+n), (m·y₂ + n·y₁)/(m+n) )

Special Cases of Section Formula

  • Midpoint Formula: When m = n = 1 (1:1 ratio), the point is the midpoint: M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
  • External Division: When the point lies outside the segment, the ratio has a negative component

Board Exam Patterns for Section Formula

Examiners frequently ask questions that require:

  • Direct application of the Section Formula
  • Finding vertices of triangles or quadrilaterals
  • Proving collinearity of three points
  • Combined problems using both Distance and Section Formulas

Example: Find the coordinates of the point that divides the line segment joining A(2, 3) and B(8, 9) in the ratio 2:1.

Solution: Using Section Formula with m=2, n=1:
P = ((2×8 + 1×2)/(2+1), (2×9 + 1×3)/(2+1)) = (18/3, 21/3) = (6, 7)

Exam Strategy: What CBSE Expects from You

(Based on CBSE Class 10 Maths Question Papers 2023-2025)

Recent CBSE board papers show that Coordinate Geometry questions typically appear as:

  • Short Answer (2 marks): Direct formula application, simple calculations
  • Long Answer (4 marks): Multi-step problems combining both formulas, proving properties, or geometric reasoning
  • Application Problems: Real-world scenarios involving plotting points and finding distances

To score well, ensure you:

  • Know both formulas by heart (not just derived, but ready to apply)
  • Show step-by-step calculations clearly
  • Label your coordinate plane when asked to plot
  • Verify your answers using alternative methods when possible
