Polynomials for CBSE Class 10 Maths: Zeroes and Coefficient Relations

Published on November 18, 2026

Polynomials form one of the fundamental topics in CBSE Class 10 Mathematics, appearing consistently in both the board examination and competitive entrance exams. Chapter 2 of the NCERT Mathematics textbook dedicates substantial focus to understanding polynomial functions, their characteristics, and the critical relationship between zeroes and coefficients. This comprehensive guide will help you master polynomials and secure maximum marks in your CBSE board exams.

Understanding Polynomials and Their Zeroes

A polynomial is an algebraic expression consisting of variables and coefficients, involving only the operations of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and non-negative integer exponents. In CBSE Class 10, you primarily work with polynomials in one variable, typically expressed as p(x) = a₀ + a₁x + a₂x² + ... + aₙxⁿ.

The zero (or root) of a polynomial p(x) is a value of x for which p(x) = 0. For example, if p(x) = x² - 5x + 6, then x = 2 and x = 3 are zeroes because p(2) = 0 and p(3) = 0.

Key Concepts from NCERT Chapter 2:

  • A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes
  • Linear polynomial (degree 1) has exactly one zero
  • Quadratic polynomial (degree 2) has at most two zeroes
  • Cubic polynomial (degree 3) has at most three zeroes

The board examination frequently tests your ability to find zeroes both algebraically and graphically. Ensure you can identify zeroes from factored forms and understand the geometric interpretation where zeroes represent x-intercepts on the graph.

Relationship Between Zeroes and Coefficients

This is the most critical section for CBSE Class 10 board exams. The relationship between the zeroes and coefficients of a polynomial is a powerful tool that appears in multiple question types.

For Quadratic Polynomials:

If α and β are the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial ax² + bx + c, then:

  • Sum of zeroes (α + β) = -b/a
  • Product of zeroes (αβ) = c/a

For example, consider the polynomial 2x² - 7x + 3. Here a = 2, b = -7, c = 3. If we find the zeroes to be 3 and 1/2, we can verify: Sum = 3 + 1/2 = 7/2 = -(-7)/2 ✓, and Product = 3 × 1/2 = 3/2 ✓

For Cubic Polynomials:

If α, β, and γ are the zeroes of the cubic polynomial ax³ + bx² + cx + d, then:

  • Sum of zeroes (α + β + γ) = -b/a
  • Sum of products taken two at a time (αβ + βγ + γα) = c/a
  • Product of zeroes (αβγ) = -d/a

⚠️ Common Mistake Alert:

Students often confuse the signs in the relationship formulas. Remember: the sum of zeroes equals negative b/a, not positive. This sign error is a frequent cause of incorrect answers in board exams. Always double-check your formula application!

Forming Polynomials from Given Zeroes

Board exams often ask you to construct polynomials given their zeroes. This is the reverse process and requires you to apply the relationships you've learned.

If you know the zeroes α and β of a quadratic polynomial, you can write: p(x) = a(x - α)(x - β), which expands to p(x) = a[x² - (α + β)x + αβ].

Worked Example:

Find a quadratic polynomial with zeroes 2 and -3.

Using the formula: p(x) = x² - (sum of zeroes)x + (product of zeroes)

= x² - (2 + (-3))x + (2 × (-3))

= x² - (-1)x + (-6)

= x² + x - 6

This straightforward approach is frequently tested in CBSE Class 10 board exams with varying difficulty levels.

Division Algorithm and Polynomial Analysis

The Polynomial Division Algorithm from NCERT Chapter 2 states: If p(x) and g(x) are two polynomials with g(x) ≠ 0, then there exist unique polynomials q(x) and r(x) such that: p(x) = g(x) × q(x) + r(x), where either r(x) = 0 or degree of r(x) < degree of g(x).

The Remainder Theorem is crucial: If p(x) is divided by (x - a), the remainder is p(a). This directly connects to zeroes—if the remainder is 0, then (x - a) is a factor and a is a zero of p(x).

CBSE board exams test this through problems asking you to verify if a given value is a zero or to find remainders without actual division. Master the Remainder Theorem as it significantly reduces calculation time.
