Surface Areas and Volumes for CBSE Class 10: Combined Solids Guide

Published: November 28, 2026

Surface Areas and Volumes is one of the most scoring yet scoring chapters in CBSE Class 10 Mathematics (Chapter 13, NCERT). This chapter deals with calculating surface areas and volumes of three-dimensional objects, particularly combined or composite solids. With consistent practice and clear conceptual understanding, students can easily score 10-12 marks in the board exam from this chapter.

Understanding Combined Solids and Their Importance

Combined solids are three-dimensional objects formed by joining two or more basic shapes like cubes, cylinders, cones, hemispheres, and spheres. In CBSE Class 10 board exams, questions often ask students to find the total surface area or volume when two solids are combined.

For example, a typical question might be: "A toy is made by placing a cone on top of a hemisphere. If the radius of the hemisphere is 7 cm and the slant height of the cone is 10 cm, find the total surface area of the toy."

Understanding combined solids is crucial because:

  • It tests your ability to identify individual shapes within a composite solid
  • It requires application of multiple formulas simultaneously
  • CBSE board exams typically include 2-3 questions (4-5 marks) from this topic
  • It bridges the gap between 2D geometry and 3D visualization skills

Key Formulas: Surface Area and Volume (NCERT Chapter 13)

Mastering these formulas is non-negotiable for scoring well. Keep these formulae handy while solving problems:

Basic Shapes - Formulas You Must Know:

  • Cube: Surface Area = 6a², Volume = a³ (where a is side)
  • Cuboid: Surface Area = 2(lw + wh + hl), Volume = l × w × h
  • Cylinder: Surface Area = 2πr(r + h), Volume = πr²h
  • Cone: Surface Area = πr(r + l), Volume = (1/3)πr²h (l is slant height)
  • Sphere: Surface Area = 4πr², Volume = (4/3)πr³
  • Hemisphere: Surface Area = 3πr² (curved + base), Volume = (2/3)πr³
⚠️ Common Mistake Alert! Many students forget to include the base area when two solids are joined. For example, when a cone sits on a hemisphere, the base of the cone (which touches the hemisphere) is NOT part of the total surface area. Only the curved surfaces count in combined solids. Always draw a diagram and identify which surfaces are exposed.

Step-by-Step Approach to Solving Combined Solids Problems

CBSE board exams expect students to show detailed working. Follow this approach for guaranteed marks:

Step 1: Identify the Component Solids

Read the problem carefully and identify which basic shapes form the combined solid. Draw a rough diagram. For instance, if the problem mentions "a cone placed on a hemisphere," you have two shapes: cone + hemisphere.

Step 2: Extract Given Information

List all given measurements. Convert units if necessary (e.g., diameter to radius). Write down what you need to find (surface area or volume or both).

Step 3: Identify Exposed Surfaces

This is critical. In a combined solid, only the exposed surfaces contribute to total surface area. Hidden surfaces (where two solids meet) are excluded. For instance, in a cone-hemisphere combination:

  • Total Surface Area = Curved Surface Area of Cone + Curved Surface Area of Hemisphere
  • The circular base of the cone (touching the hemisphere) is NOT included
  • The flat circular base of the hemisphere (bottom) is included if it's exposed

Step 4: Apply Formulas and Calculate

Use the standard formulas for each component and add them. Use π = 22/7 or 3.14 as instructed. Show all calculation steps for full marks.

Step 5: Write the Final Answer

Always include units (cm², cm³, m², etc.) and double-check your arithmetic.

Exam Pattern and Expected Questions (CBSE Board)

Based on previous CBSE Class 10 board exams and the pattern specified in NCERT, here's what to expect:

  • Short Answer Questions (2 marks): Simple volume or surface area of single solids or basic combined solids
  • Long Answer Questions (4 marks): Complex combined solids problems requiring multi-step calculations
  • Very Long Answer Questions (5 marks): Real-world applications like making toys, containers, or structures from combined shapes

Frequently appearing topics:

  • Cone + Hemisphere combinations (toy/top designs)
  • Cylinder + Cone combinations (container designs)
  • Sphere + Hemisphere or Cube variations
  • Volume calculations when liquid is poured from one shape to another
  • Cost or material problems based on surface area

Pro Tip: CBSE loves asking problems about "toys made by joining two solids" or "containers made of different materials." These are your golden questions—practice similar problems from NCERT Exercise 13.1, 13.2, and 13.3 thoroughly.
