Metals and Non-Metals for CBSE Class 10: Properties and Reactions

Published on November 04, 2026

Metals and Non-Metals is one of the most important chapters in CBSE Class 10 Science (Chapter 3 of the NCERT textbook). This chapter forms the foundation for understanding chemical properties, reactivity series, and various chemical reactions that frequently appear in board exams. Whether you're preparing for your periodic test or the final board exam, mastering this topic is essential for scoring well in Science.

Understanding the Basics: Physical Properties of Metals and Non-Metals

The first step to excelling in this chapter is distinguishing between metals and non-metals based on their physical properties. According to the NCERT Class 10 Science curriculum, metals and non-metals exhibit distinctly different characteristics that help us identify them.

Physical Properties of Metals

  • Luster: Metals are shiny and reflective, which is why they're often used for decorative purposes
  • Malleability: Metals can be beaten into thin sheets without breaking (e.g., gold, aluminum)
  • Ductility: Metals can be drawn into thin wires (e.g., copper, silver)
  • Conductivity: Both thermal and electrical conductivity is high in metals
  • Melting Point: Generally high melting points (except mercury, which is liquid at room temperature)
  • Density: Most metals are dense and heavy, though some like aluminum are relatively light

Physical Properties of Non-Metals

  • No Luster: Non-metals are generally dull and not reflective
  • Brittleness: Non-metals are brittle and break easily when hammered
  • Poor Conductivity: Most non-metals are poor conductors of heat and electricity (except graphite)
  • Low Melting Points: Most non-metals have low melting points (e.g., sulfur, phosphorus)
  • Variable States: Non-metals exist as solids, liquids, and gases at room temperature
⚠️ Common Exam Mistake: Students often forget that carbon (as diamond) shows properties of non-metals despite being hard. Remember: the key distinguishing factor is conductivity and chemical reactivity, not just hardness. Diamond is a non-metal because it doesn't conduct electricity (in its pure form) and doesn't exhibit metallic properties.

Chemical Properties and Reactivity: NCERT Chapter 3 Deep Dive

The chemical properties of metals and non-metals are equally important and form the backbone of many board exam questions. These properties determine how elements behave in chemical reactions and their position in the reactivity series.

Chemical Properties of Metals

According to NCERT Class 10, metals exhibit the following key chemical properties:

  • Reaction with Oxygen: Metals react with oxygen to form oxides (e.g., 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO). Some metals like sodium react vigorously, while others like gold don't react easily.
  • Reaction with Water: Reactive metals like sodium and potassium react vigorously with water (2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑), while less reactive metals like iron react with steam.
  • Reaction with Dilute Acids: Metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series displace hydrogen from dilute acids (Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑).
  • Displacement Reactions: More reactive metals can displace less reactive metals from their salt solutions (e.g., Cu + 2AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag).

Chemical Properties of Non-Metals

  • Reaction with Oxygen: Non-metals form acidic or neutral oxides (e.g., C + O₂ → CO₂; 2S + O₂ → 2SO₂)
  • No Reaction with Water: Non-metals generally don't react with cold water, though some like chlorine react with water to form acids
  • Reaction with Metals: Non-metals gain electrons from metals to form ionic compounds (2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl)

The Reactivity Series: Your Board Exam Essential Guide

The reactivity series is one of the most frequently tested concepts in CBSE Class 10 Science exams. It's a list of metals arranged in decreasing order of their reactivity. Understanding this series helps you predict which reactions will occur and which won't.

The Reactivity Series (in decreasing order): K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au

How to use the Reactivity Series:

  • A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from its salt solution
  • Metals above hydrogen in the series react with dilute acids to produce hydrogen gas
  • Metals below hydrogen cannot displace hydrogen from acids
  • This series is crucial for predicting single displacement reactions, a common board exam question type

Corrosion, Rusting, and Prevention: Real-World Applications

CBSE Class 10 Science also focuses on practical applications of metal and non-metal properties. Corrosion and rusting are important concepts that appear in both theory and practical exam questions.

Rusting of Iron

Rusting is the corrosion of iron when exposed to oxygen and moisture. The equation for rust formation is: 4Fe + 3O₂ + 2xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃.xH₂O (rust)

Prevention Methods

  • Painting or Coating: Prevents direct contact with oxygen and moisture
  • Galvanization: Coating iron with zinc to protect it
  • Alloying: Creating stainless steel by mixing iron with chromium and nickel
  • Lubrication: Applying oil or grease as a protective layer
