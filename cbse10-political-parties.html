Political Parties: Functions and Challenges

Published on October 25, 2026

Political parties form the backbone of democratic governance. For CBSE Class 10 Civics students, understanding political parties, their functions, and the challenges they face is crucial for both board exams and civic awareness. This chapter, typically covered in Unit 3 of the CBSE Civics curriculum, explores how political parties operate in India's democratic framework.

What is a Political Party? (NCERT Chapter 5)

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in government. In India's democratic system, political parties play a vital intermediary role between citizens and the state.

Key characteristics of a political party include:

  • Common ideology: Members share similar political beliefs and values
  • Organizational structure: Defined hierarchy from national to grassroots levels
  • Election participation: Contest in elections to form the government
  • Policy formulation: Develop political programs and manifestos
  • Representation: Represent interests of specific sections or broader constituencies

India has a multi-party system with national parties like the Indian National Congress (INC) and Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), as well as numerous regional and state parties. This makes Indian democracy unique and ensures diverse representation.

Functions of Political Parties (Exam Focus Area)

Political parties perform several essential functions in a democratic society. CBSE board exams frequently test students' understanding of these functions in 2-3 mark and 5-mark questions.

1. Contest Elections: The primary function of parties is to contest elections at various levels—national, state, and local. They field candidates and campaign to secure votes.

2. Form Government: The party or coalition that wins the majority forms the government. In India, the party with the largest number of Lok Sabha seats typically forms the national government.

3. Make Policies and Laws: Once in power, political parties implement their electoral promises through legislation. They shape the nation's policies on education, health, economy, defense, and social welfare.

4. Provide Opposition: Parties that don't form the government play a crucial role as the Opposition. They scrutinize government policies, highlight failures, and provide alternative viewpoints in Parliament.

5. Aggregate Interests: Political parties bring together diverse interests of various groups and communities. They consolidate these interests into a coherent political program.

6. Political Socialization: Parties educate citizens about political issues, rights, and duties. They mobilize citizens to participate in the democratic process.

7. Policy Implementation: Through their elected representatives, parties implement policies and laws, and hold the executive accountable to the legislature.

⭐ Common Exam Mistake to Avoid

Students often confuse the functions of political parties with the functions of pressure groups or NGOs. Remember: Only political parties contest elections and form governments. Pressure groups influence policies without directly contesting elections. This distinction is crucial for distinguishing between 2-3 mark questions on your board exam.

Challenges Faced by Political Parties in India (NCERT Chapter 5)

While political parties are essential for democracy, they face significant challenges in contemporary India. Understanding these challenges demonstrates critical thinking—important for scoring high marks in long-answer questions.

1. Internal Democracy and Leadership Issues: Many parties lack internal democratic processes. Power often concentrates in the hands of a single leader or family, limiting internal debate and decision-making at grassroots levels. This weakens party discipline and member participation.

2. Money Power in Elections: Electoral campaigns have become increasingly expensive. Large funding from corporate houses and wealthy individuals creates concerns about corruption and favoritism. This raises questions about whether policies favor the wealthy donors rather than common citizens.

3. Criminalization of Politics: A growing number of candidates with criminal backgrounds contest elections. This compromises the integrity of the political system and public trust in democratic institutions.

4. Weak Organizational Structure: Many parties, especially regional ones, lack proper organizational structure. This results in irregular member participation, inadequate training of party workers, and inconsistent message delivery.

5. Coalition Politics Complications: In India's multi-party system, coalition governments are common. Smaller parties demand disproportionate ministerial positions, leading to instability and compromised governance.

6. Dynastic Politics: Many major parties are dominated by political families. This limits opportunities for talented individuals outside these families and reduces meritocracy within parties.

7. Policy-Free Elections: Some parties focus on personalities, regional identities, or religious considerations rather than substantive policy debates. This hampers informed voter choice.

Types of Political Parties in India

CBSE exams often include questions about classifying parties by their reach and ideology:

National Parties: These contest elections across multiple states and have significant all-India presence. Examples include INC, BJP, NCP, and CPI(M).

State Parties/Regional Parties: These operate primarily in specific states or regions. Examples include TMC (West Bengal), DMK (Tamil Nadu), and Shiv Sena (Maharashtra).

Parties by Ideology: Some parties are secular (support separation of religion and state), while others are communal (based on religious identity). This classification helps understand their policies and voter base.

Important Points for Board Exam Preparation

5-Mark Question Format: Expect questions like "Explain any five functions of political parties" or "Discuss the challenges faced by political parties in India." Structure your answers with clear headings and examples from the Indian context.

Case Study Format: Examiners may present a scenario involving party politics and ask students to analyze it using concepts from the chapter. Always reference specific examples like the 2019 or 2024 elections, coalition governments, or recent political developments.

Comparison Questions: You might encounter questions comparing roles of political parties vs. pressure groups or national vs. state parties. Create comparison tables for effective learning.

Source-Based Questions: Some questions may include newspaper excerpts or policy documents. Read carefully and answer based on the source and your textbook knowledge.
