Areas Related to Circles: Sector and Segment Formulas

Published on November 27, 2026

Areas related to circles is one of the most critical chapters in CBSE Class 10 Mathematics, appearing consistently in board exams with 4-6 marks allocated. This chapter builds upon your knowledge of circles from earlier classes and introduces you to calculating areas of sectors, segments, and combined shapes. Understanding these formulas and their applications is essential for scoring well in the mathematics board examination.

Understanding Circles, Sectors, and Segments (NCERT Chapter 12)

Before diving into formulas, it's crucial to understand the fundamental definitions from the NCERT textbook:

  • Circle: A collection of all points equidistant from a center point
  • Radius (r): The distance from the center to any point on the circle
  • Sector: A region of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc (think of a pizza slice)
  • Segment: A region between a chord and the arc it subtends
  • Arc Length: The distance along the curved edge of a circle

CBSE exams frequently ask questions involving these definitions, so clarity on terminology is your first step toward mastery.

Key Formulas: Sector Area and Arc Length

The sector formulas are the backbone of this chapter. NCERT Chapter 12 emphasizes these standard formulas that you must memorize and understand:

Sector Area Formula:
Area of sector = (θ/360°) × πr²

Where θ is the central angle in degrees and r is the radius.
Arc Length Formula:
Arc length (l) = (θ/360°) × 2πr

Or alternatively: l = rθ (when θ is in radians)

⚠️ Common Mistake Alert!

Students often confuse sector area with segment area. Remember: a sector includes the two radii (looks like a pie slice), while a segment is just the curved part (looks like a curved cap). Always verify which one the question is asking for!

Practical Examples from Board Exams

Example 1: Find the area of a sector with radius 7 cm and central angle 60°.

Solution: Area = (60°/360°) × π × 7² = (1/6) × π × 49 = (49π/6) cm² ≈ 25.67 cm²

Example 2: A circle has radius 10 cm. Find the arc length of a sector with central angle 45°.

Solution: Arc length = (45°/360°) × 2π × 10 = (1/8) × 20π = (5π/2) cm ≈ 7.85 cm

Segment Area and Advanced Calculations (NCERT Section 12.2)

Segment area is slightly more complex and often appears in higher-mark questions. A segment is bounded by a chord and the arc it subtends. To find the segment area, you must subtract the triangle area from the sector area.

Segment Area Formula:
Area of segment = Area of sector - Area of triangle
Area of segment = (θ/360°) × πr² - (1/2) × r² × sin(θ)

For θ in degrees, this can also be written as:
Area of segment = r² × [(θ × π/360°) - sin(θ)/2]
Board Exam Tip: Questions involving segments are usually 4-5 mark questions. Always draw a clear diagram showing the chord, arc, center, and central angle. This visual representation helps prevent errors and shows clear understanding to the examiner.

Step-by-Step Solution Approach

  1. Identify the radius and central angle from the problem statement
  2. Determine whether you need sector area, segment area, or arc length
  3. For sectors: directly apply the area formula
  4. For segments: calculate both sector area and triangle area, then subtract
  5. Always express your final answer in the same units as the given measurements
  6. When using π, follow the convention given in your exam (often π = 22/7 or π = 3.14)

Combined Shapes and Real-World Applications

CBSE Class 10 board exams frequently test your ability to apply these formulas to combined shapes. You might encounter problems involving:

  • Circles inscribed or circumscribed around squares and rectangles
  • Shaded regions formed by overlapping circles and polygons
  • Composite shapes where you must add or subtract multiple sector and segment areas
  • Real-world scenarios like pizza slices, clock sectors, or garden designs

For these problems, the key strategy is to break the complex shape into simpler components (sectors, segments, triangles, rectangles) that you can calculate individually, then combine them appropriately.

📌 Key Board Exam Pattern

The CBSE typically includes one 2-mark question (basic sector/arc calculation), one 3-mark question (combining sector and triangle), and one 5-mark question (complex combined shapes or real-world application). Practice all three difficulty levels!

Using Correct π Values

Different questions specify different values for π:

  • When instructed to use π = 22/7: preferred for fraction-based problems
  • When instructed to use π = 3.14: preferred for decimal approximations
  • When no instruction given: use π = 22/7 (standard CBSE convention)

Practice Questions for Self-Assessment

To truly master this chapter, solve these representative questions from NCERT and previous board exams:

  1. A circle has radius 14 cm. Find the area of a sector subtending an angle of 90° at the center.
  2. Find the area of a segment of a circle of radius 10 cm, if the central angle is 120°.
  3. The length of an arc of a circle with radius 5 cm is 10 cm. Find the area of the sector formed by this arc.
  4. A square of side 4 cm is inscribed in a circle. Find the area of the circle and the area of one of the four segments between the circle and the square's sides.
  5. Two circles with radii 5 cm and 3 cm are concentric. Find the area of the ring (annulus) between them, and then calculate the area of a sector of the ring subtending 60° at the center.
