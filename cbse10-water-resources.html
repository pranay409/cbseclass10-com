Water Resources for CBSE Class 10: Dams and Rainwater Harvesting

Published on October 18, 2026

Water is one of the most crucial natural resources on Earth, essential for survival, agriculture, and industrial development. For CBSE Class 10 Geography students, understanding water resources management, particularly through dams and rainwater harvesting, is vital for both board exams and real-world awareness. This comprehensive guide covers the NCERT-grounded concepts you need to master.

NCERT Chapter 5: Water ResourcesIntroduction to Water Resources

Water resources include surface water (rivers, lakes, oceans) and groundwater. India is endowed with vast water resources, yet there's a persistent challenge of equitable distribution and management. The CBSE Class 10 Geography syllabus emphasizes how human intervention through infrastructure like dams plays a crucial role in water management.

According to NCERT, approximately 70% of Earth's surface is covered with water, but only about 2.5% is freshwater. Of this freshwater, about 69% is locked in ice caps and glaciers, leaving only about 0.3% accessible for human use. This scarcity makes efficient water resource management absolutely essential for sustainable development.

Role of Dams in Water Resource Management

NCERT Chapter 5: Water Resources - Focus on Multipurpose Projects

Dams are constructed barriers across rivers designed to hold back water and create reservoirs. They serve multiple purposes, which is why they're often called "multipurpose projects" in CBSE textbooks. The main functions include:

  • Water Supply: Dams provide water for irrigation, drinking, and industrial use
  • Hydroelectric Power Generation: The force of water creates electricity for millions of people
  • Flood Control: Dams regulate river flow and prevent devastating floods
  • Recreation: Reservoirs behind dams attract tourists and provide recreational facilities
  • Navigation: They make rivers navigable for transportation

India has constructed some of the world's largest dams for multipurpose use. Examples commonly appearing in board exams include the Bhakra-Nangal Dam, the Damodar Valley Project, and the Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO) projects. These dams have significantly contributed to India's economic development and agricultural productivity.

Common Exam Mistake:

Students often confuse the purposes of dams with the purposes of canals. Remember: Dams create water storage and manage flow, while canals distribute stored water. Both work together in irrigation systems. On board exams, clearly distinguish between these two when answering questions about water management infrastructure.

Rainwater Harvesting: A Sustainable Solution

NCERT Chapter 5: Water Resources - Groundwater Depletion & Solutions

Rainwater harvesting is the process of collecting, storing, and using rainwater for various purposes. This ancient practice has regained importance in modern India due to declining groundwater levels and increasing water scarcity. The CBSE Class 10 curriculum emphasizes rainwater harvesting as a critical strategy for sustainable water management.

There are two primary methods of rainwater harvesting taught in NCERT:

  1. Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting: Water falling on roofs is collected through pipes and gutters into storage tanks or ground. This method is popular in urban areas and residential complexes. The water can be used for domestic purposes or recharged into groundwater.
  2. Catchment Area Treatment: Entire catchment areas are treated to increase water infiltration into the ground. This method is used in rural areas and includes measures like contour bunding, percolation tanks, and check dams.

India has traditional rainwater harvesting systems in various regions. For example, in Kerala, people use "Kunds" for water collection; in Rajasthan, "Johads" serve the same purpose; and in Tamil Nadu, "Tanks" are traditional water bodies that collect rainwater. These systems demonstrate India's historical understanding of water conservation and are important topics for CBSE board exams.

Rainwater harvesting offers numerous advantages: it reduces groundwater depletion, increases groundwater levels during rainy seasons, reduces urban flooding, decreases pollution from surface runoff, and provides an alternative water source for arid and semi-arid regions. These benefits are commonly asked in 3-mark and 5-mark questions on board exams.

Key Exam Topics and Important Points

CBSE Class 10 Board Exam Pattern - Geography Section B (Resource and Development)

For CBSE Class 10 board exams, focus on these high-frequency topics:

  • Multipurpose Projects: Understand what makes a dam "multipurpose" and name at least 3-4 major Indian projects
  • Benefits and Problems of Dams: Board exams often ask for both advantages (irrigation, power, flood control) and disadvantages (displacement of people, environmental impact)
  • Types of Rainwater Harvesting: Differentiate between rooftop and catchment area methods with examples
  • Methods of Groundwater Recharge: Be familiar with percolation tanks, check dams, johads, and contour bunding
  • Water Scarcity Causes: Know factors like uneven distribution, population growth, agricultural demand, and climate change
  • Conservation Strategies: Beyond dams and rainwater harvesting, understand drip irrigation, watershed management, and water recycling

Expect 1-2 questions on water resources in the Geography section of your CBSE Class 10 board exam. These may be in the form of 1-mark definition questions, 3-mark descriptive questions, or 5-mark long-answer questions. Maps showing major dams and water resource distribution often appear in map-based questions.
