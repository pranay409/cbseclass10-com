Circles for CBSE Class 10: Tangent Theorems and Proofs Explained

Published on November 26, 2026

Introduction: Circles are one of the most important topics in CBSE Class 10 Mathematics, appearing consistently in board exams with 4-6 marks allocated. Chapter 10 of the NCERT textbook focuses on tangent-related theorems and their proofs, which form the foundation for understanding circle geometry. This comprehensive guide covers all essential tangent theorems, their proofs, and practical exam applications to help you score maximum marks.

Understanding Tangents: Definition and Basic Concepts

(NCERT Class 10, Chapter 10)

A tangent to a circle is a line that touches the circle at exactly one point, known as the point of tangency. Unlike secants which intersect the circle at two points, tangents maintain a unique relationship with circles that is crucial for understanding higher geometry.

Key Definitions

  • Tangent: A line that touches the circle at one point
  • Point of Contact: The single point where the tangent touches the circle
  • Radius to Point of Tangency: Always perpendicular to the tangent line
  • Tangent Segment: The line segment from an external point to the point of tangency
⚠️ Common Mistake: Students often confuse tangents with chords or secants. Remember: a tangent touches the circle at exactly ONE point from outside, while a chord connects two points on the circle, and a secant intersects the circle at two points.

Theorem 1: Radius is Perpendicular to Tangent

(NCERT Class 10, Chapter 10, Theorem 1)

This is the most fundamental theorem: The radius drawn to the point of contact of a tangent is perpendicular to the tangent.

Proof:

Given: Circle with center O, tangent l at point T

To Prove: OT ⊥ l

Proof: Assume OT is not perpendicular to l. Draw a perpendicular from O to l, meeting it at point P (where P ≠ T). Then OP < OT. Since OT is the radius and OP < OT, point P lies inside the circle. This means the line l passes through P, which is inside the circle, and must intersect the circle at two points, contradicting the definition of tangent. Therefore, our assumption is wrong, and OT must be perpendicular to l.

Exam Application:

This theorem is used to solve problems involving tangent-radius angles, finding distances, and constructing perpendiculars. Expect 1-2 mark questions testing this concept directly.

Theorem 2: Tangent Segments from External Point

(NCERT Class 10, Chapter 10, Theorem 2)

A critical theorem for board exams: The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Proof:

Given: Circle with center O, external point P, tangents PA and PB touching at A and B respectively

To Prove: PA = PB

Proof: In triangles OAP and OBP:

  • OA = OB (radii of the same circle)
  • ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (radius perpendicular to tangent)
  • OP = OP (common side)
By RHS congruence, △OAP ≅ △OBP. Therefore, PA = PB (CPCT).

📌 Exam Pattern: This theorem appears in 2-3 mark questions where you need to find perimeters of triangles, prove equal segments, or solve optimization problems. Practice applying it with quadrilaterals circumscribed around circles.

Theorem 3: Tangent-Chord Angle Theorem

(NCERT Class 10, Chapter 10, Extended Concept)

An advanced concept: The angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact equals half the arc subtended by the chord.

Mathematical Statement:

If PT is a tangent at T and TA is a chord, then ∠PTA = (1/2) × (arc TA)

Key Applications in Exams:

  • Finding angles in circles without direct angle measurements
  • Proving properties of cyclic quadrilaterals with tangents
  • Solving complex 4-5 mark problems combining multiple theorems

Related Concept: Alternate Segment Theorem

The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment. This is frequently tested in CBSE board exams, especially in problems involving tangent-secant combinations.

Solving Board Exam Questions: Practical Strategy

CBSE Class 10 board exams typically include:

  • 1-mark questions: Definition-based, direct application of perpendicularity
  • 2-mark questions: Tangent segment equality, simple proofs
  • 3-4 mark questions: Combined theorems, perimeter calculations, proof by construction
  • 5-6 mark questions: Complex geometry combining circles, tangents, and other shapes
💡 Pro Tip: Always draw clear diagrams when solving tangent problems. Mark all given information, radii, and right angles explicitly. This helps visualize relationships and prevents logical errors during proofs.

Must-Practice Problem Types

To excel in board exams, master these problem categories:

  • Finding the length of tangent segments given radius and distance from center
  • Proving tangent properties in quadrilaterals (squares, rhombuses inscribed in circles)
  • Calculating perimeters using tangent segment equality
  • Solving real-world applications like roller design, pipe connections, and architectural designs
