Chemical Reactions and Equations: Complete Guide for CBSE Class 10

📅 Published on November 2, 2026 | ⏱️ 8-10 minute read

Chemical reactions and equations are fundamental concepts in CBSE Class 10 Science that form the foundation for your board exams. Chapter 1 of your Science textbook introduces you to the language of chemistry—how substances transform through chemical reactions and how we represent these changes using equations. Mastering this chapter is crucial as it's a stepping stone to understanding more complex topics in chemistry.

This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about chemical reactions and equations, aligned with the NCERT curriculum and board exam patterns. Let's dive into the key concepts that will help you score well in your board exams.

Understanding Chemical Reactions: What They Are and Why They Matter

A chemical reaction is a process where one or more substances (reactants) are transformed into one or more new substances (products). During a chemical reaction, the chemical composition of matter changes, which is different from physical changes. The bonds between atoms break and new bonds form, creating entirely new substances with different properties.

According to the NCERT Class 10 Science textbook (Chapter 1), chemical reactions are everywhere in nature—from rusting of iron to photosynthesis in plants. For board exam purposes, you need to understand that:

  • Reactants are the starting substances that participate in the reaction
  • Products are the new substances formed after the reaction
  • Chemical bonds break in reactants and new bonds form in products
  • Chemical reactions are often irreversible or difficult to reverse

The board exams typically ask you to identify whether a change is chemical or physical, and to explain the difference with examples. Common examination questions focus on recognizing signs of chemical reactions such as heat release, color change, gas formation, or precipitate formation.

Types of Chemical Reactions: Classification for Board Exams

NCERT Class 10 covers four main types of chemical reactions that are heavily emphasized in board exams. Understanding these types is essential for solving numerical problems and explaining reaction mechanisms.

1. Combination Reactions

A combination reaction occurs when two or more substances combine to form a single product. The general format is: A + B → AB. Examples include:

  • Burning of carbon: C + O₂ → CO₂
  • Formation of ammonia: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃
  • Rusting of iron: 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃

2. Decomposition Reactions

In decomposition reactions, a single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances. The format is: AB → A + B. Common examples from NCERT include:

  • Thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
  • Electrolysis of water: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂
  • Decomposition of silver bromide (used in photography): 2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂

3. Displacement Reactions

A displacement reaction involves one element displacing another from a compound. They follow the pattern: A + BC → AC + B. This includes both single and double displacement reactions. Examples include:

  • Zinc displacing copper: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
  • Iron displacing copper: Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
  • Double displacement: AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃

4. Redox Reactions (Oxidation-Reduction)

Redox reactions involve the transfer of electrons between substances. An element that loses electrons is oxidized, while an element that gains electrons is reduced. These reactions are fundamental to understanding chemistry and appear frequently in board exams.

⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid

Many students confuse displacement reactions with redox reactions. Remember: all displacement reactions are redox reactions, but not all redox reactions are displacement reactions. Also, don't forget to check the activity series of metals when predicting displacement reactions. A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from its salt solution.

Balancing Chemical Equations: The Essential Skill for Board Exams

One of the most important skills for CBSE Class 10 board exams is balancing chemical equations. According to the law of conservation of mass, the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of the equation.

Steps to balance a chemical equation:

  1. Write the unbalanced equation with chemical formulas of reactants and products
  2. Count atoms of each element on both sides
  3. Use coefficients (whole numbers placed before formulas) to balance
  4. Check your work by counting atoms again on both sides
  5. Ensure coefficients are in the lowest ratio (simplify if needed)

Let's work through an example from NCERT:

Unbalanced: Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃
Balanced: 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃

Board exams often include questions where you need to balance complex equations involving multiple elements. Practice is key to mastering this skill. Start with simple equations and gradually move to more complex ones involving polyatomic ions.

Writing and Interpreting Chemical Equations: What Examiners Expect

Beyond just balancing, NCERT requires students to understand the notation used in chemical equations. Board exam questions may ask you to interpret symbols and conditions in equations:

  • → represents the direction of the reaction (yields)
  • Δ indicates heating or application of heat
  • (s), (l), (g), (aq) represent states: solid, liquid, gas, aqueous solution
  • ↓ indicates formation of a precipitate
  • ↑ indicates release of a gas
