Real Numbers for CBSE Class 10 Maths: Euclid's Division Lemma Explained

Published on November 17, 2026

Euclid's Division Lemma is one of the most fundamental concepts in CBSE Class 10 Maths Chapter 1 (Real Numbers). It forms the foundation for understanding the Euclidean algorithm and plays a crucial role in board exams. This comprehensive guide will help you master this essential topic with clarity and confidence.

Understanding Euclid's Division Lemma

Euclid's Division Lemma is a mathematical principle that relates division and remainders. According to the lemma, for any two positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q (quotient) and r (remainder) such that:

a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b

Here, a is the dividend, b is the divisor, q is the quotient, and r is the remainder. The condition 0 ≤ r < b ensures that the remainder is always non-negative and strictly less than the divisor.

This lemma is not just an abstract mathematical concept—it's the backbone of the Euclidean algorithm, which is used to find the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD) of two numbers. Understanding this principle is vital for scoring well in CBSE board exams, where questions often test your ability to apply this concept in practical scenarios.

⚡ Key Tip for Board Exams

Always remember: 0 ≤ r < b is essential. The remainder must be non-negative and strictly less than the divisor. Many students make the mistake of forgetting this condition, which can lead to incorrect applications of the lemma.

Application: Finding HCF Using Euclidean Algorithm (NCERT Chapter 1)

The practical application of Euclid's Division Lemma is in finding the HCF (Highest Common Factor) or GCD (Greatest Common Divisor) of two numbers through the Euclidean algorithm. This is a favorite topic in CBSE board exams, often appearing as 2-3 mark questions.

Steps to find HCF using Euclidean Algorithm:

  1. Apply Euclid's Division Lemma repeatedly to the given numbers
  2. Continue until the remainder becomes zero
  3. The last non-zero remainder is the HCF of the two numbers

Example: Find HCF of 1071 and 462

  • 1071 = 462 × 2 + 147
  • 462 = 147 × 3 + 21
  • 147 = 21 × 7 + 0

Since the remainder is 0, the HCF of 1071 and 462 is 21.

This algorithmic approach is elegant and efficient, especially when dealing with large numbers where finding factors manually would be tedious. Board exams frequently test this method, so practice multiple examples until the process becomes second nature.

Exam Pattern & Expected Questions (Based on NCERT Analysis)

CBSE Class 10 board exams typically include the following types of questions related to Euclid's Division Lemma:

  • Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks): Apply Euclidean algorithm to find HCF of two given numbers
  • Application-based Problems: Real-world scenarios like dividing objects, distributing items equally, or finding maximum group sizes
  • Proof-based Questions: Show that two numbers are coprime using Euclid's Division Lemma
  • Multiple Step Problems: Combine Euclidean algorithm with LCM concepts or other real number properties

The board exam often tests not just mechanical application but also conceptual understanding. Questions may ask you to explain why the algorithm works or use it to solve indirect problems. This is where deep understanding, beyond rote memorization, becomes crucial.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Many CBSE Class 10 students make preventable errors while working with Euclid's Division Lemma. Here are the most common pitfalls:

  • Ignoring the remainder constraint: Forgetting that 0 ≤ r < b can lead to multiple valid answers for the same problem
  • Switching dividend and divisor: Always divide the larger number by the smaller one initially
  • Stopping prematurely: Continue the algorithm until the remainder is exactly zero—not just until you get a small remainder
  • Arithmetic errors: Double-check your multiplication and subtraction in each step, as errors compound
  • Not relating to real-world context: Remember that this lemma represents actual division with quotient and remainder

Practice with varied examples to internalize the process and avoid these errors during your board exams. Time management is also critical—these problems should be solved quickly once you're comfortable with the method.
