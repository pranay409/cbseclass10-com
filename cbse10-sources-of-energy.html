Sources of Energy for CBSE Class 10: Renewable vs Non-Renewable

Published on November 15, 2026

Energy is the foundation of modern civilization. For CBSE Class 10 Science, understanding sources of energy is crucial for both your board exams and real-world applications. Chapter 14 of the NCERT Science textbook focuses on "Sources of Energy," which is a significant part of your curriculum. This comprehensive guide will help you master renewable and non-renewable energy sources with exam-focused insights.

Understanding Energy Sources: The NCERT Framework

Energy sources are broadly classified into two categories: renewable and non-renewable. According to NCERT, this classification is based on whether an energy source can be regenerated naturally within a reasonable time frame or depleted over time.

Non-renewable energy sources are those that cannot be replenished at the rate at which they are consumed. These are finite and deplete over time. Renewable energy sources are naturally replenished and can be used sustainably without exhausting them.

The CBSE board emphasizes understanding the pros and cons of each source, environmental implications, and their applications in India. Your exam will test not just definition knowledge but conceptual understanding of why we need to shift towards renewable energy.

Non-Renewable Energy Sources: Past and Present

Non-renewable sources have powered human civilization for centuries. These include fossil fuels and nuclear energy. Understanding their advantages and limitations is essential for scoring full marks in Section A (1-mark questions) and Section B (3-mark questions) of your CBSE board exam.

Energy Source Characteristics Advantages Disadvantages
Coal Fossil fuel, oldest resource Abundant, reliable power generation High pollution, greenhouse gas emissions, non-renewable
Petroleum Liquid fossil fuel High energy density, versatile Expensive, environmental impact, limited reserves
Natural Gas Gaseous fossil fuel Cleaner than coal, efficient Non-renewable, methane emissions
Nuclear Energy Energy from uranium fission High energy output, zero emissions Radioactive waste, safety concerns, high cost

⚠️ Common Exam Mistake to Avoid

Students often confuse "non-renewable" with "polluting." While most non-renewable sources are polluting, nuclear energy is non-renewable but produces zero emissions during operation. The CBSE board often asks: "Can nuclear energy be considered completely clean?" Answer: It's emission-free but creates radioactive waste. This distinction frequently appears in 3-mark questions.

Renewable Energy Sources: The Sustainable Future

Renewable energy sources are the focus of modern energy policies in India and globally. The NCERT textbook extensively covers solar energy, wind energy, hydroelectric power, biomass, and geothermal energy. For CBSE exams, you must understand each source's mechanism, efficiency, and applications in India.

Solar Energy: India is one of the world's leading solar power producers. The sun provides enormous energy through radiation. Solar panels convert this into electricity (photovoltaic effect). Key advantages: free, abundant, and environmentally clean. Disadvantages: intermittent (depends on weather), high initial cost, requires large space.

Wind Energy: Wind turbines convert kinetic energy of wind into electrical energy. India has significant wind power capacity, especially in Tamil Nadu and Gujarat. Advantages: renewable, no emissions, low operating cost. Disadvantages: depends on wind speed, visual impact, bird mortality concerns.

Hydroelectric Power: Water flowing from dams drives turbines to generate electricity. This is India's largest renewable energy source. Advantages: reliable, no fuel cost, flood control benefits. Disadvantages: environmental impact on aquatic ecosystems, displacement of people, seasonal variation.

Biomass and Biogas: Organic matter is burned or decomposed to produce energy. India uses agricultural waste and animal dung extensively. Advantages: uses waste material, reduces pollution. Disadvantages: lower efficiency, creates smoke if not properly managed.

Geothermal Energy: Heat from Earth's interior is used to generate electricity. Limited application in India due to geological conditions, but increasingly relevant in volcanic regions.

CBSE Board Exam Patterns and Expected Questions

Based on previous years' CBSE papers, here are common question patterns you should prepare for:

  • 1-Mark Questions: "Define renewable energy" or "Name any two non-renewable sources." These test basic definitions.
  • 3-Mark Questions: "Explain how solar energy is converted to electricity" or "Compare coal and wind energy." These require detailed explanations with advantages/disadvantages.
  • 5-Mark Questions: "Discuss why India should shift from non-renewable to renewable energy sources" or "How does hydroelectric power work? What are its limitations?" These assess comprehensive understanding and analytical skills.
  • Diagram-Based Questions: You may be asked to label diagrams of a solar panel, wind turbine, or hydroelectric dam. Practice drawing and labeling these components.

✅ Key Board Exam Tip

Always structure your answers with: (1) Definition/explanation, (2) How it works or characteristics, (3) Advantages, (4) Disadvantages, (5) Real-world application in India. This comprehensive approach ensures you score full marks in 3-mark and 5-mark questions.

The energy crisis is central to CBSE's curriculum philosophy. Your textbook emphasizes that India must balance energy security with environmental sustainability. Be ready to discuss India's renewable energy targets, the role of government initiatives like the International Solar Alliance, and India's coal dependence and transition strategies.

Understanding sources of energy isn't just about passing exams—it's about comprehending one of humanity's greatest challenges. With systematic study of NCERT Chapter 14, regular practice of previous years' papers, and conceptual clarity, you'll excel in this topic.
