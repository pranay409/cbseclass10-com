Arithmetic Progressions for CBSE Class 10: Nth Term and Sum Formulas

Published on November 21, 2026

Arithmetic Progressions (AP) is one of the most important and scoring topics in the CBSE Class 10 Mathematics curriculum. This chapter introduces the concept of sequences, specifically arithmetic progressions, and teaches you how to find any term in a sequence and the sum of terms. Mastering this chapter is essential not only for board exams but also for competitive exams like JEE and NEET. Let's dive deep into understanding APs with crystal clarity.

Understanding Arithmetic Progressions (NCERT Chapter 5)

An Arithmetic Progression is a sequence of numbers where the difference between any two consecutive terms is constant. This constant difference is called the Common Difference (d).

Definition: A sequence a₁, a₂, a₃, ... aₙ is called an AP if aₙ₊₁ - aₙ = d for all n ≥ 1, where d is a constant.

Example: The sequence 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, ... is an AP with first term a = 2 and common difference d = 3.

To identify whether a sequence is an AP, check if the difference between consecutive terms remains constant throughout. This is a fundamental concept that appears frequently in CBSE board exams.

Reference: NCERT Mathematics Class 10, Chapter 5: Arithmetic Progressions (Sections 5.1-5.2)

The Nth Term Formula of an AP

The nth term (also called the general term) of an AP can be found using the formula:

Nth Term Formula

aₙ = a + (n - 1)d

Where:

  • aₙ = nth term of the AP
  • a = first term of the AP
  • n = position of the term
  • d = common difference
Example: Find the 10th term of the AP: 3, 7, 11, 15, ...

Here, a = 3, d = 4, n = 10
a₁₀ = 3 + (10 - 1) × 4 = 3 + 36 = 39

So, the 10th term is 39.

This formula is crucial for board exams. Students often need to find specific terms or solve problems where certain terms are given, and they must find the common difference or first term. Practice deriving and using this formula from multiple angles.

Reference: NCERT Mathematics Class 10, Chapter 5: Arithmetic Progressions (Section 5.3)

Sum of n Terms of an AP (Critical for CBSE Exams)

To find the sum of the first n terms of an AP, we use the Sum Formula. This is one of the most frequently asked questions in CBSE board exams, appearing in both 2-mark and 5-mark questions.

Sum of n Terms Formula

Method 1: Sₙ = n/2 × (2a + (n - 1)d)

Method 2: Sₙ = n/2 × (a + l)

Where:

  • Sₙ = sum of first n terms
  • n = number of terms
  • a = first term
  • d = common difference
  • l = last term (aₙ)
Example: Find the sum of the first 15 terms of the AP: 2, 6, 10, 14, ...

Here, a = 2, d = 4, n = 15
Sₙ = 15/2 × (2(2) + (15 - 1) × 4)
Sₙ = 15/2 × (4 + 56) = 15/2 × 60 = 450

So, the sum of the first 15 terms is 450.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Using aₙ = a + nd instead of aₙ = a + (n - 1)d. Remember, the exponent is (n - 1), not n.

Mistake 2: Confusing the two sum formulas. Use Sₙ = n/2 × (2a + (n - 1)d) when you know a and d. Use Sₙ = n/2 × (a + l) when you know the first and last terms.

Mistake 3: Not checking if the given sequence is actually an AP before applying formulas. Always verify that the common difference is constant.

Practical Applications and Exam Pattern

The CBSE board exam typically includes 2-3 questions on Arithmetic Progressions, with varying difficulty levels:

Question Type Marks Typical Pattern
Find nth term / Common Difference 1-2 Direct application of formula
Sum of n terms problems 2-3 Multi-step calculation
Word problems on AP 5 Real-life scenarios requiring AP concept
Board Exam Sample: An arithmetic progression has first term 5 and common difference 3. How many terms of this AP must be added to get a sum of 450?

Given: a = 5, d = 3, Sₙ = 450
450 = n/2 × (2(5) + (n - 1) × 3)
450 = n/2 × (10 + 3n - 3)
900 = n(7 + 3n)
3n² + 7n - 900 = 0
Solving this quadratic equation gives n = 15 (taking the positive value)
