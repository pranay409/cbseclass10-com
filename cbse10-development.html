Development for CBSE Class 10 Economics: Income and Other Criteria

Published on October 27, 2026

Development is one of the most important concepts in CBSE Class 10 Economics curriculum. Chapter 1 of your NCERT textbook introduces the idea that development is not just about income but involves multiple dimensions that affect the quality of life. Understanding this concept is crucial for scoring well in both board exams and competitive entrance tests.

Understanding Development: Beyond Income

[NCERT Chapter 1: Development]

When we talk about development, students often assume it's purely about earning more money or having a higher income. However, development is a much broader concept. According to your NCERT textbook, development refers to the process of growth, progress, and positive change in the economic and social well-being of individuals and nations.

The key insight here is that development cannot be measured by income alone. Consider this: a person might earn a high income but lack access to clean drinking water, quality education, or healthcare facilities. Are they truly developed? The answer is no. This is why economists and international organizations use multiple indicators to assess development.

Development involves improvements in various sectors including health, education, infrastructure, employment, environment, and social security. It's about creating conditions where every individual can realize their potential and live a dignified life with access to basic amenities.

Income as a Development Criteria

[NCERT Chapter 1: Income and Other Criteria]

While income is not the only measure of development, it remains a significant criterion. Income levels determine purchasing power—a person's ability to buy essential goods and services necessary for survival and well-being. Higher income generally allows for better nutrition, housing, healthcare, and education.

In the CBSE syllabus, you'll learn that countries are often classified by their income levels:

  • Low-income countries: Countries with very low Per Capita Income (PCI)
  • Middle-income countries: Countries with moderate PCI levels
  • High-income countries: Developed nations with high PCI

The World Bank often uses Per Capita Income (PCI)—calculated by dividing the total national income by the population—as a benchmark for classification. However, this is merely a starting point. Two countries with similar PCI might have vastly different development levels due to variations in how that income is distributed and utilized for public welfare.

📌 Key Exam Tip: Common Mistake to Avoid

Students often write that "development = high income" in their answers. This is incorrect and will cost you marks. Always remember: development is MULTIDIMENSIONAL. Income is ONE criterion among many. Always mention that development depends on education, health, infrastructure, and social indicators, not just income alone.

Other Important Development Criteria

[NCERT Chapter 1: Development Criteria]

Your CBSE Class 10 Economics syllabus emphasizes that development must be evaluated using multiple criteria. Here are the most important ones you need to know:

  • Health and Life Expectancy: This measures the average lifespan of people in a country. Higher life expectancy indicates better healthcare facilities, nutrition, and living conditions.
  • Education: Literacy rates and enrollment in schools/colleges reflect a population's access to education. This is crucial for human capital development and economic growth.
  • Infrastructure: Availability of roads, electricity, communication networks, and public transportation indicates development level and supports economic activities.
  • Employment and Working Conditions: Access to decent jobs with fair wages and safe working conditions is essential for development.
  • Infant Mortality Rate (IMR): The number of infants who die before reaching one year of age. Lower IMR indicates better healthcare and development.
  • Gender Equality: Equal opportunities and rights for women and men in education, employment, and decision-making.
  • Environmental Quality: Access to clean water, air, and sustainable resources is part of holistic development.

HDI and the Human Development Index

[NCERT Chapter 1: HDI Concept]

The Human Development Index (HDI) is a composite statistic used by the United Nations to measure development across countries. Your textbook uses HDI as a framework to demonstrate why income alone is insufficient for measuring development.

The HDI combines three key dimensions:

  • Health: Measured by life expectancy at birth
  • Education: Measured by adult literacy rate and enrollment in schools
  • Income: Measured by Per Capita Income in PPP (Purchasing Power Parity) terms

Countries are ranked globally on their HDI scores. This ranking often differs from income-based rankings, proving that a wealthy nation might lag in health or education, and vice versa. For example, a country with high PCI might have poor literacy rates or high infant mortality, affecting its overall development status.

Understanding HDI is essential for board exams. You should be able to explain why countries with similar incomes can have different development levels, and why development requires investment across multiple sectors simultaneously.

💡 Board Exam Question Pattern

Expect 2-3 mark questions asking: "Why can't development be measured by income alone?" or "What are the different criteria used to measure development?" Also prepare for 3-5 mark questions requiring you to compare development indicators between countries or discuss specific criteria like HDI, literacy, and life expectancy.

Mastering this chapter is fundamental because development concepts appear across multiple CBSE Class 10 Economics chapters. Whether studying globalization, trade, or sustainability, you'll need solid foundational knowledge about what development truly means.
