Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

Published on November 19, 2026

The chapter on Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables is one of the fundamental topics in CBSE Class 10 Mathematics. This concept bridges algebra and real-world applications, making it essential not just for board exams but also for competitive exams. In this comprehensive guide, we'll cover everything you need to master this topic.

What are Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables? NCERT Ch. 3

A linear equation in two variables is an equation of the form ax + by + c = 0, where a, b, and c are real numbers, and a and b are not both zero. When we have two such equations together, we call them a pair of linear equations in two variables.

For example:

  • 2x + 3y = 8
  • 3x - 2y = 5

These equations are written in the standard form: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0. The main goal is to find values of x and y that satisfy both equations simultaneously.

Methods to Solve Pair of Linear Equations NCERT Ch. 3.2-3.5

1. Graphical Method

In the graphical method, we plot both equations on a coordinate plane. The point where the two lines intersect is the solution to the pair of equations. This method is useful for understanding the geometric interpretation but is less practical for exact solutions.

Possible cases:

  • Intersecting lines: One unique solution exists (consistent and independent)
  • Parallel lines: No solution exists (inconsistent)
  • Coincident lines: Infinite solutions exist (consistent and dependent)

2. Substitution Method

In this method, we solve one equation for one variable and substitute it into the other equation. This is particularly useful when one equation has a coefficient of 1 for either variable.

Steps:

  • Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation
  • Substitute this expression in the second equation
  • Solve the resulting single-variable equation
  • Back-substitute to find the other variable

3. Elimination Method

The elimination method (also called the method of equating coefficients) involves multiplying equations by suitable constants to make coefficients equal, then adding or subtracting to eliminate one variable.

Steps:

  • Make coefficients of one variable equal by multiplying equations by suitable numbers
  • Add or subtract the equations to eliminate that variable
  • Solve the resulting equation
  • Substitute back to find the other variable

⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid

Many students forget to multiply both sides of the equation when making coefficients equal in the elimination method. Remember: if you multiply one side by a number, you MUST multiply the other side by the same number. Otherwise, your solution will be incorrect.

Consistency and Consistency Analysis NCERT Ch. 3.3

To determine whether a pair of linear equations has a solution without solving it, we use the ratios of coefficients:

For the pair: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0

  • If a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂: The lines intersect at exactly one point → Unique solution (Consistent)
  • If a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂: The lines are parallel → No solution (Inconsistent)
  • If a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂: The lines coincide → Infinite solutions (Consistent and dependent)

This analysis is crucial for CBSE board exams, as questions often ask you to determine consistency before or instead of actually solving the equations.

Board Exam Patterns and Tips CBSE Exam Pattern

Based on recent CBSE Class 10 board exam patterns, here's what you should expect:

  • 1-2 mark questions: Usually ask for consistency analysis or simple substitution/elimination
  • 3-4 mark questions: Word problems requiring you to form equations and solve them
  • 5-6 mark questions: Complex word problems with multiple steps and consistency checks

Exam-specific tips:

  • Always check your answer by substituting back into both original equations
  • Show all steps clearly—this helps you get method marks even if the final answer is wrong
  • Use the elimination method for equations with larger coefficients, as it's faster
  • For word problems, clearly define variables and state your equations before solving
  • Practice consistency analysis problems—they appear frequently in board exams
