Globalisation and the Indian Economy

Published on October 30, 2026

Globalisation is one of the most important chapters in the CBSE Class 10 Economics curriculum. This chapter from the Social Science textbook explores how the Indian economy has been increasingly integrated into the global economy, the consequences of this integration, and its impact on Indian society, workers, and consumers. Understanding globalisation is crucial not only for your board exams but also for comprehending contemporary economic issues.

Understanding Globalisation: Definition and Concepts (NCERT Chapter 4)

Globalisation refers to the process of rapid integration of different economies of the world through the flow of goods, services, investment, technology, and information across national borders. It is a phenomenon driven by technological advancement, liberalisation of trade policies, and increased foreign investment.

Key Features of Globalisation

  • Trade Liberalisation: Removal of tariffs and trade barriers, allowing free movement of goods and services
  • Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): Investment by foreign companies in domestic sectors
  • Technological Transfer: Access to global technology and innovation
  • Labour Mobility: Movement of skilled and unskilled workers across borders
  • Integration of Markets: Global financial and commodity markets becoming interconnected

For CBSE exams, you must be able to define globalisation and explain how it differs from internationalisation. Globalisation emphasises the breaking down of barriers and the creation of truly global systems, whereas internationalisation refers to increasing economic interactions between countries while maintaining national distinctions.

Impact of Globalisation on the Indian Economy

Positive Impacts

  • Economic Growth: India's GDP growth accelerated post-1991 economic liberalisation, with an average growth rate of 6-7% during the 2000s
  • Increased Exports: Indian IT services, pharmaceuticals, textiles, and agricultural products found global markets, generating foreign exchange
  • Foreign Investment: MNCs (Multinational Corporations) established manufacturing and service units in India, creating employment
  • Technology Transfer: Access to global technology improved productivity across sectors
  • Consumer Choice: Indian consumers gained access to a wider variety of products at competitive prices
  • Infrastructure Development: Globalisation prompted investment in ports, airports, roads, and telecommunications

Negative Impacts and Challenges

  • Unemployment: Increased competition from imports led to closure of domestic industries, especially in textiles and agriculture
  • Agricultural Crisis: Cheap agricultural imports displaced farmers; suicides among farmers increased in the 1990s-2000s
  • Inequality: Benefits of globalisation were concentrated in urban areas and among the educated elite; rural and marginalised populations were left behind
  • Cultural Impact: Local industries and traditional occupations declined due to competition from global products
  • Environmental Concerns: Increased industrial activity led to pollution and resource depletion
  • Dependence: Heavy reliance on imports created trade deficits and currency pressures

⚠️ Common Exam Mistake

Students often view globalisation as entirely positive or entirely negative. Remember: globalisation has mixed effects. For board exams, always discuss both benefits and drawbacks. CBSE questions often ask you to analyse the impact critically, evaluating which sections of society benefit and which suffer.

Role of Multinational Corporations (MNCs) in Globalisation (NCERT Focus)

MNCs are companies that operate in multiple countries, producing and selling goods and services worldwide. They are key agents of globalisation. In India, MNCs have invested heavily in sectors like IT, automobiles, pharmaceuticals, and retail.

How MNCs Function in India

  • Outsourcing: Companies like Infosys, TCS, and Wipro provide IT services to global clients, creating millions of jobs
  • Manufacturing: MNCs like Hyundai, Maruti Suzuki, and Samsung manufacture in India for both domestic and export markets
  • Retail: Companies like Walmart and Amazon operate e-commerce platforms in India
  • Benefits: Employment, technology transfer, capital inflow, and integration with global value chains
  • Concerns: Profit repatriation, exploitation of workers, environmental degradation, and displacement of local businesses

For CBSE Class 10 exams, you should be familiar with case studies of MNCs operating in India. Common examples include Coca-Cola, Nike's manufacturing partners, and IT giants. Be prepared to discuss both their contributions and the criticisms they face.

Trade Agreements and India's Integration into Global Economy

India's participation in global trade has been shaped by various agreements and policy changes. The most significant was the Economic Liberalisation of 1991, which opened India's economy to foreign trade and investment. This was followed by India's membership in the World Trade Organization (WTO) in 1995.

Key Policy Changes

  • Removal of License Raj: Industrial licensing requirements were abolished, allowing easier business operations
  • Tariff Reduction: Import duties were significantly reduced, exposing Indian industries to global competition
  • Foreign Direct Investment: Sectors like retail, telecommunications, and insurance were opened to FDI
  • Export Promotion: Special Economic Zones (SEZs) were created to boost exports

CBSE exam questions often ask about the effects of these policy changes. Be ready to discuss how liberalisation affected different sectors differently—some sectors like IT and pharmaceuticals thrived, while traditional sectors like textiles and agriculture struggled initially.

Important Exam Tips and Key Takeaways

Concepts to Master for Board Exams

  • Define globalisation: Be precise and mention integration of economies, flow of goods/services/capital
  • Distinguish between export and import: Exports generate foreign exchange; imports can displace domestic industries
  • Know the role of WTO: It facilitates global trade and resolves trade disputes
  • Understand the concept of comparative advantage: Why countries trade based on their relative efficiencies
  • Case studies: Be familiar with at least 2-3 real examples of how globalisation affected Indian sectors

Likely CBSE Exam Questions

Based on CBSE patterns, expect questions like:

  • "Explain how globalisation has affected the Indian textile industry."
  • "Discuss the positive and negative impacts of MNCs on the Indian economy."
  • "What role did the 1991 economic reforms play in globalisation?"
  • "How has globalisation affected Indian farmers?"
  • "Explain the concept of outsourcing with examples from India."
