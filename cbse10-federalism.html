Federalism for CBSE Class 10 Civics: Indian Federal System Explained

Published: October 24, 2026

Federalism is one of the most important concepts in the CBSE Class 10 Civics syllabus, specifically covered in Chapter 2 of the NCERT textbook "Democratic Politics - II". Understanding the Indian federal system is crucial for both board exams and developing a deeper knowledge of how our Constitution functions. This comprehensive guide will help you master federalism with exam-focused explanations and real-world examples.

What is Federalism? (Chapter 2 - NCERT Foundation)

Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority (the national government) and constituent political units (states). The term "federal" comes from the Latin word "foedus," which means covenant or contract.

In simple terms, federalism means sharing power. The Indian Constitution establishes a federal structure where:

  • Union Government (Central Government): Handles national issues like defense, foreign policy, and currency
  • State Governments: Manage state-specific matters like education, police, and agriculture
  • Local Governments: Address municipal and village-level concerns

This three-tier system ensures that no single authority becomes too powerful, protecting democratic principles and respecting regional diversity—a critical characteristic emphasized in CBSE Class 10 Civics exams.

Key Features of Indian Federalism (NCERT Chapter 2 - Board Exam Focus)

1. Two Levels of Government

The Indian federal system initially operated with two levels: Union and States. However, the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments (1992) introduced a third tier—Local Government (Panchayats and Municipalities)—making it a three-tier federal system. This is frequently asked in board exams.

2. Written Constitution

India's federal structure is defined and protected by the Constitution. The Constitution clearly demarcates powers between the Union and States through three lists:

  • Union List: 97 subjects (defense, foreign affairs, railways, currency)
  • State List: 66 subjects (police, education, agriculture, health)
  • Concurrent List: 52 subjects (both Union and States can make laws—marriage, education, forests)

3. Rigidity of the Constitution

The Constitution cannot be easily amended. Changes to federal provisions require special procedures, ensuring the federal structure remains stable and protected from arbitrary changes by any single government.

4. Independent Judiciary

The Supreme Court acts as a neutral arbiter to resolve disputes between the Union and States, protecting federalism by interpreting constitutional provisions fairly.

5. Bicameral Parliament

The Rajya Sabha represents states in Parliament, ensuring state interests are considered in national legislation. This is a distinctive feature of Indian federalism often tested in Class 10 board exams.

⭐ Common Exam Mistake to Avoid

Students often confuse "Federalism" with "Decentralization." Remember: Federalism is a constitutional division of power between different levels of government, while decentralization is the process of transferring power from higher to lower levels. India practices both—federalism between Union-States, and decentralization through the three-tier system established by the 73rd Amendment.

Distribution of Powers: Union vs. States (NCERT - Most Tested Topic)

Board exams frequently ask questions about which government has authority over specific subjects. Here's a quick reference guide:

Union Government Powers (Union List):

  • National Defense and Armed Forces
  • Foreign Affairs and International Relations
  • Currency and Banking
  • Railways and Interstate Commerce
  • Atomic Energy and Postal Services
  • Income Tax and Customs Duty

State Government Powers (State List):

  • Public Order and Police
  • Primary and Secondary Education
  • Agriculture and Irrigation
  • Local Government and Municipalities
  • Trade and Commerce within State
  • Property Rights and Contract Laws

Concurrent Powers (Both can legislate):

  • Criminal and Civil Law
  • Marriage and Divorce
  • Adoption and Wills
  • Education (including universities)
  • Social Security and Welfare
  • Environmental Protection

When both Union and States make laws on concurrent subjects, the Union law supersedes the State law if there's a conflict. This doctrine is called "supremacy of Union law" and is an important exam concept.

Cooperative Federalism & Inter-governmental Relations

Chapter 2 of NCERT emphasizes that Indian federalism is not purely competitive but cooperative. Union and States work together through various mechanisms:

Mechanisms of Cooperative Federalism:

  • Finance Commission: Recommends revenue sharing between Union and States (appears every 5 years)
  • National Development Council: Coordinates planning between Union and States
  • Inter-State Council: Resolves disputes and facilitates cooperation
  • Prime Minister's Council of Ministers: Includes state representatives in policy-making
  • Concurrent List Subjects: Require coordination between both levels

Understanding cooperative federalism is crucial for scoring well in board exams, as questions often ask how Union and States balance power in specific policy areas.

The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments strengthened cooperative federalism by introducing Local Government as a third tier, ensuring power reaches the grassroots level. This decentralization of power is essential for participatory democracy and often features in long-answer questions.
